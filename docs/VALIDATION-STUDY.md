# Validation study: do caregivers agree on what a moment means?

LuminAAC suggests what a non-speaking child might mean. Before we claim those suggestions are useful, we need to know how often the people who know the child best agree with each other. If two caregivers agree only by chance, no model can do better than chance either.

## What the app does
- In The Bridge, open Insights, then **Check how well we agree**.
- Each caregiver types a name and labels saved moments one at a time. They can't see the app's guess or anyone else's answer.
- When a caregiver finishes, they see percent agreement and Cohen's kappa for each pair of people. "Not sure" answers are left out.
- **Download anonymous results** gives a CSV with scrambled moment ids, rater letters (A, B, …), the cue list and the app's guess. It has no names, notes, recordings, places or times.

## Protocol
1. Recruit 10–20 families, each with at least two caregivers who see the child most days.
2. Get written consent from each adult, and the child's assent where they can give it. Explain that the downloaded file has no names, but the family chooses whether to share it.
3. Over two weeks, the family saves moments as usual.
4. At the end, each caregiver labels every moment on their own, without discussing it.
5. The family sends the CSV to the study team by a channel they choose. The study team never gets the app data.
6. Analyse kappa per family, and app guess against the majority label.

## Reading kappa
| Kappa | Meaning |
|---|---|
| under 0.2 | slight |
| 0.2–0.4 | fair |
| 0.4–0.6 | moderate |
| 0.6–0.8 | substantial |
| over 0.8 | almost perfect |

A target for "the app's guesses are worth showing" is app-against-majority kappa at or above caregiver-against-caregiver kappa.

## Before recruiting
- Ethics review (an IRB or equivalent) if results will be published.
- A plain-language consent form and a way to withdraw.
- Not a medical device: results describe communication, not diagnosis.
